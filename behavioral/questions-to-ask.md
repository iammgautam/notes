## Why it matters

- "Do you have any questions?" is part of the evaluation. "No" reads as low interest.
- It's also your best chance to find out whether you want the job.
- Ask 2–3 per round. Tailor to the interviewer's role.

## Rules

- Ask about things you can't find on their website.
- Open questions ("how", "what", "tell me about"), not yes/no.
- Listen to the answer and ask one follow-up. That's the conversation they'll remember.
- Save compensation and leave policy for the recruiter or HR, not the engineering rounds.

## For engineers (technical rounds)

- What does a typical week look like for someone on this team?
- How does code get from a PR to production? How long does that usually take?
- What's the hardest technical problem the team is working on right now?
- How do you handle database migrations and schema changes on large tables?
- How is on-call organized? How many pages does a typical shift get?
- What does observability look like: logs, metrics, tracing, error tracking?
- When something breaks in production, what happens next? Do you write postmortems?
- What part of the codebase would you rewrite if you had a month?

## For the hiring manager

- What would success look like for this role in the first 3 and 6 months?
- What's the biggest challenge the team is facing this year?
- How are priorities decided between features, tech debt and reliability?
- How do engineers interact with customers or product?
- How do you support growth: feedback cadence, promotions, ownership of bigger projects?
- Why is this role open?
- What do the strongest people on your team do differently?

## For senior leadership / skip-level

- Where do you see the product going in the next year or two?
- What's the biggest risk to that plan?
- How does engineering influence product direction?
- What do you wish engineers here did more of?

## For HR / recruiter

- What are the next steps and the timeline?
- How is the team structured, and who would I work with most?
- Questions on compensation, notice period, remote/hybrid policy and benefits go here.

## Matched to your background

Questions that also signal relevant experience (Postgres, Django, multi-tenant SaaS, background jobs, LLM work):

- How do you isolate tenants: shared schema, schema per tenant, database per tenant? Any noisy-neighbor problems?
- What do you use for background jobs, and what pain points have you hit with it?
- Where are the main performance bottlenecks today: database, external APIs, something else?
- How are you using LLMs in the product today? How do you evaluate output quality and handle provider outages?
- How much of the codebase is legacy, and is there a plan for modernizing it?

## Red-flag detectors

Listen for vague answers to these:

- How often do people get paged outside working hours?
- How long have people on the team been here?
- What happened the last time a project missed its deadline?
- How are disagreements on technical direction resolved?

## Avoid

- Anything answered on the careers page or in the job description.
- "What does your company do?"
- Asking about salary or time off in a technical round.
- "Did I get the job?" or "How did I do?"
- Questions that sound like complaints about past employers ("Do you micromanage?").

## Closing line

After your questions, a short close works:

```text
Thanks, this was useful. The <specific thing they mentioned> sounds like
exactly the kind of problem I enjoy working on. Looking forward to next
steps.
```
